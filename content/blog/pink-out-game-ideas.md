Every October, gyms and fields turn pink for a night. The best pink out games are not the ones with the most pink. They are the ones where a team knows exactly who they are playing for, and the people in the stands can tell.

Here is how to plan one that people remember, with ideas you can use whether you have four weeks or four days.

## What a pink out is, and what it is for

A pink out game is a regular-season game dedicated to breast cancer awareness. Teams wear pink, fans wear pink, and the night raises money or attention for people affected by the disease.

The "for" matters more than the "what". A pink out with a name attached to it, a mom on the team, a coach's sister, a teacher at the school, lands differently than one that is only a color scheme. Start there. Ask the team who they are playing for. Almost every roster has an answer.

## Twelve ideas that work

**1. Dedicate the night to one person, out loud.** Announce their name before the anthem. Put it on the program. If they can be there, walk them out with the captains. If they have passed, invite their family to sit with the team.

**2. Pink bands for the whole roster.** A pink band on every wrist reads across a gym in a way t-shirts do not, and the team keeps wearing them after the night is over. If you use prayer bands, each one opens to a page where the team can leave a prayer for the person you are honoring, and that person can read every one of them on their phone. Our breast cancer awareness band is ribbon pink with a plum and rose theme.

**3. A "we play for" list.** Give every player a card at practice: "I play for ______." Read the names during warmups, or print them on the back of the warmup shirts. Nobody forgets the person they wrote down.

**4. Survivor recognition at halftime.** Invite survivors from your school and community onto the court or field. Keep it short, give each one a band or a flower, and let the crowd stand. This is often the moment people talk about afterward.

**5. A band table at the gate.** Sell or give away pink bands as people come in, with a sign that says who the night is for. If the bands are prayer bands, put the tap instructions on the sign, and people can pray for your honoree from their seats before the game starts.

**6. A team prayer circle.** Set up a private prayer circle for the team and share the join code at practice. Post the honoree's name and the specific thing to pray for, and let players add their own prayers under it through October. Parents and grandparents who cannot make the game can join too.

**7. Pink on the opposing team.** Send the other team pink bands or ribbons ahead of time and invite them to wear pink too. It turns a rivalry night into a shared one, and their fans usually join in.

**8. A donation match.** Ask a local business to match the gate or the band table up to a set amount. Say so on the announcement and the business gets its name on the night.

**9. Chalk the walk.** Let players and fans write names in pink chalk on the sidewalk into the gym or stadium. By tipoff the walk is covered.

**10. The photo wall.** A pink backdrop and a sign, "Tonight we play for ______," with the blank filled in by whoever steps up. Fans post them, and the name travels further than the game did.

**11. Senior night, folded in.** If pink out lands near senior night, let seniors pick the honoree or read the names. It gives the older players ownership of the night.

**12. The after.** The next practice, ask what the honoree said. If they read the prayers on their band page, tell the team. The night is the start, not the end.

## A four-week plan

**Four weeks out.** Pick the date and the honoree. Ask their permission and what they are comfortable with. Order bands and any shirts; allow time for shipping.

**Three weeks out.** Announce it to families and the school. Line up the donation match and the halftime moment. Invite survivors.

**Two weeks out.** Bands arrive. Hand them out at practice and let the team write their prayers or notes for the honoree. Send the opposing team theirs.

**One week out.** Confirm the announcer script, the halftime timing, the photo wall, and who staffs the gate table. Post the "we play for" cards.

**Game day.** Set up early. Put the honoree's name where people can see it from the parking lot. Play the game.

## If you only have a few days

Skip the shirts. Get bands on wrists, pick the name, announce it, and do the halftime moment. Those four things carry the night. Everything else is extra.
