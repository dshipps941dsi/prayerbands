Church recovery ministries run on Friday nights and small groups. The meeting is strong. The worship is strong. The problem is the six days in between, when the person who shared on Friday is alone with the thing they shared about.

This is a plan for the long middle: a band on the wrist, a private circle the group leader runs, and a way to keep first names first.

## Who this is for

Celebrate Recovery groups, church-run twelve-step meetings, grief and divorce recovery groups, and any ministry where people come back weekly and carry something heavy between visits. If your group is part of a program with its own traditions about outside materials, follow those. This is written for ministries that already use their own resources.

## The problem the meeting cannot solve

A small group does two things well: it lets people be honest, and it lets them be heard. What it cannot do is be there on Wednesday.

Most ministries try to close that gap with a group text. Group texts work for about three weeks, then they become noise, then someone leaves them, then the leader stops posting. What lasts is a rhythm with a leader at the front of it and a low bar to show up.

## The setup

**One band per person.** A silicone band with a verse on the outside and a chip inside. The verse is the group's verse for the season. Nothing on the outside names recovery; a coworker sees a band with a verse on it. Bands come in sizes and colors, so a table of twelve does not look like a uniform.

**One private circle per group.** A prayer circle is a private group with a leader and a shared wall. The group leader and a co-leader run it. Only they post topics, so the wall does not become a feed. Every member can tap Pray under a topic, and anyone with a band can write a prayer under it.

**The join code, in the room.** Show the code on the screen or hand it out on a card. Members tap their band, make a free account, and enter the code. It takes about a minute, and you can do it together at the end of the first night.

**First names first.** Accounts need a name, and a first name is enough. Prayers on the wall show the first name the person chose. Their private journal is theirs alone. The band itself has no battery and no location, and it never knows who is wearing it.

## The weekly rhythm

**Friday night.** The leader posts the week's topic after the meeting, tied to what was shared, without naming anyone's story. "For everyone who spoke about a relationship tonight." Members get a notification and tap Pray on the way home.

**Monday.** A second topic if the group needs one. Short. "For the first hard day of the week."

**Wednesday.** The leader taps Pray on every prayer written under the week's topics. Members see the count climb. That is the "someone is still here" signal, and it costs the leader two minutes.

**Friday, before the meeting.** The leader marks answered what can be marked answered, and says so at the meeting.

That is the whole rhythm. One or two posts a week from the leader. Nobody has to write anything to take part; tapping Pray is participation.

## What the band does that the app alone does not

The circle works with no band at all. The band matters for the person who will not open an app on Wednesday. They look down, the band is there, they tap it, the page opens, and the circle is one tap further. For the group, the band is the thing that says, without a word, "I am part of something on Friday nights," in a way that only they can read.

A member can also pass their band on. When someone new comes on a Friday, the person who has been there a year hands them theirs, with a note inside. The new person taps into the same story. That is what a ministry looks like when it is working.

## Privacy, plainly

- The band holds one web address and nothing else. It does not track anyone.
- A prayer on the public wall can be anonymous or first name and last initial. The group circle is private and never public.
- The journal is private to the person who writes it.
- We never sell anyone's information. The [privacy policy](/privacy) says so in detail.

If a member wants no digital footprint at all, the band is still a band with a verse on it. Nothing requires them to tap it.
