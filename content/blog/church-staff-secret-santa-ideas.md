The church staff Christmas party has one gift problem every year: the $20 limit. Below it, everything is a candle or a mug. Above it, somebody feels bad. Here is a list that stays under the limit, works for a Secret Santa or a white elephant swap, and does not end up in the church lost-and-found by Epiphany.

## First, decide which game you are playing

- **Secret Santa** means you know who you are buying for. Buy for the person. Personal beats clever.
- **White elephant** means nobody knows who ends up with it. Buy for the room. Clever beats personal, and the gift has to make sense to anyone who steals it.

The list below marks which game each idea suits.

## Under $20

**1. A prayer band with the team's verse.** Secret Santa or white elephant. A silicone band with a verse on it and a chip inside; tap it with a phone and its prayer page opens. For a staff swap, the whole team ends up with the same verse on their wrists and one page where they pray for each other through the year. It is the only gift on this list that is still doing something in March.

**2. Good coffee, the bag they would not buy themselves.** Both. Nobody steals a mug; everyone steals the coffee.

**3. A verse card set for the office.** Secret Santa. Twelve cards, one a month, for the desk.

**4. The nice pen.** Secret Santa, for the person who writes. Pastors sign a lot of things.

**5. Hand warmers and a beanie.** White elephant, for a church with a parking team.

**6. A $15 gift card to the lunch place across the street.** Both. Plain, and the most-stolen gift in the room.

**7. A small plant that survives an office.** Secret Santa. A pothos forgives everything.

**8. The church's own merch, if it is good.** White elephant. If it is not good, this is how you find out.

**9. A board game for the break room.** White elephant. Something that plays in fifteen minutes.

**10. Nice socks with a verse reference stitched small.** Secret Santa. Yes, socks. The good kind.

**11. A framed photo from this year's biggest Sunday.** Secret Santa, for a staff member who was in it.

**12. A second band to give away.** Both. Give the band, and a second one with a note: "For someone you are praying for." It turns a swap gift into a ministry moment.

## Four rules that keep a white elephant friendly

1. **Three steals and it is frozen.** Nobody wants to watch the same gift bounce for ten minutes.
2. **No gag gifts over the limit.** The joke is fine; the price tag is the problem.
3. **Open everything, every time.** The game dies when gifts stay wrapped.
4. **The person who brought the band gets to explain it.** A tap on a phone in the middle of a swap is a better demo than any card.

## For a small group instead of staff

Everything above works, with one change: skip the white elephant. Small groups know each other too well to steal from each other. Draw names, set the limit at $20, and let the band be the group's gift to itself, one per person, with the group's verse. The prayer page becomes the group's wall for the year.
