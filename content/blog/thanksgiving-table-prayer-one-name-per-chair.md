The Thanksgiving prayer usually falls to one person. Dad, Grandpa, the pastor in the family. Everyone else bows their head and waits for the turkey. It is fine, and it is over in forty seconds, and nobody at the table said anything.

Here is a version where everyone speaks, nobody has to perform, and the quiet ones get the easiest job.

## One name per chair

Before anyone sits down, put a name card at each place. Not the name of the person sitting there. The name of someone that person will pray for.

- For the kids: a teacher, a coach, a friend.
- For the adults: the relative who is not at the table this year, the neighbour who is alone, the coworker going through it.
- For the one who never prays out loud: the name of someone easy. Grandma. A cousin. They only have to say the name.

When it is time to pray, go around the table. Each person says the name on their card and one sentence. "God, be with Mrs. Patel." That counts. The whole thing takes three minutes with twelve people, and every chair took part.

## Why it works

Most people are not afraid of praying. They are afraid of praying well, in front of family, with the gravy getting cold. A name and a sentence removes the performance. There is nothing to get wrong.

It also puts thanks where it belongs. Thanksgiving prayers drift toward a list of what we have. One name per chair turns the table outward, toward twelve people who are not in the room.

## Three ways to make it stick

**Collect the cards.** After dinner, gather the name cards in a bowl. That is your family's prayer list for December. The Advent plan in [One Name a Day](/blog/advent-prayer-plan-for-families) starts with exactly this list.

**Text the names.** Someone at the table has every number. Before pie, send a text to each person named: "You were prayed for at our table today." It takes five minutes and it is the best thing that happens to some of them all week.

**Put the band on the wrist.** If your family wears prayer bands, tap one at the table and open the family's page. Each person taps Pray on it as they say their name. The count goes up in front of everyone, and when Grandma opens her band on Friday, she sees that twelve people prayed over it on Thursday.

## For the host

You do not have to announce any of this. Put the cards out. When the moment comes, say, "This year we are each going to say one name," and go first with yours. The table will follow. The quiet uncle will say his name, and that will be the sentence people remember.
