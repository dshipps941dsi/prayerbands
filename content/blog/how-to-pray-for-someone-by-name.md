Most of us want to pray for the people we love. Most of us also know how quickly "I'll pray for you" turns into a good intention we forgot by Thursday. The gap isn't sincerity. It's that a general wish to pray has nothing to hold onto.

Praying for someone *by name* closes that gap. It turns a vague hope into a specific act you can actually do, and repeat. Here is a way to do it that holds up past the first week.

## Start with one name, not a list

A list of twelve people feels faithful and produces nothing, because nobody prays through a list. Pick one person for this week. You can add a second next week if the first one held.

Say their name out loud when you pray. Not "bless everyone at church," but "Lord, be with Sarah today." Naming is what makes it real, both to you and, if you believe prayer matters, to the One you're speaking to.

## Ask for one specific thing

"Bless them" is where prayers go to die. Instead, ask for the thing that is actually on their mind:

- the surgery on Tuesday
- the job interview
- the marriage they mentioned quietly
- the son who stopped calling

If you don't know what they're carrying, that's your first prayer: "Show me how to pray for Sarah." Then ask her. People are far more willing to tell you what they need than we expect, especially when you're clearly going to do something with the answer.

## Tie it to something you already do

A new habit rarely survives on its own. It survives when it's attached to an old one. Some that work:

- **Coffee.** First sip, first name.
- **The commute.** One person per red light, or the same person the whole drive.
- **Putting on the same thing every morning.** A ring, a watch, a wristband. The object becomes the reminder, and you never have to remember to remember.

The point is to make the reminder physical and automatic. Willpower is a terrible alarm clock.

## Keep it short enough to keep

A prayer you can say in twenty seconds, every day, does more over a month than a long prayer you manage once. "Father, hold Sarah through Tuesday. Give her peace, and give the surgeon steady hands. Amen." Done. If you have more time, take it. If you don't, that was enough.

## Let them know

This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that changes the most.

Telling someone "I prayed for you this morning" is not bragging. It is one of the most encouraging things a person can hear, because it means they were on someone's mind before the day started. It also makes the prayer harder to abandon: once Sarah knows you're praying, you will keep praying.

Keep it plain. A text that says "Praying for you and Tuesday" is better than a paragraph. If you have a Prayer Band, tap **Pray** beside their name on the Partners tab and add a line. It reaches them as a notification with your name on it, and it stays in their inbox for the days when they need to reread it.

## When you miss a day

You will. Don't restart the streak or apologize to anyone. Just say the name again tomorrow. The goal was never a perfect record. The goal was a person who was prayed for far more than they would have been otherwise, and that's already true.

## A simple week

If you want a shape to follow, try this:

1. **Sunday:** choose the person. Ask what they need, if you can.
2. **Monday through Saturday:** the same short prayer, tied to the same daily moment.
3. **Wednesday:** tell them you're praying. One line.
4. **Next Sunday:** keep them, add someone, or hand the week to someone new.

That's the whole method. One name, one need, one moment a day, and one message so they know. It's small enough to do and specific enough to matter.
