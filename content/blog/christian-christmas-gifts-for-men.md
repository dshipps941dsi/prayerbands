Every family has one. Ask him what he wants for Christmas and he says "nothing," and he means it. He has the socks. He has the mug. He does not want another devotional he will not open.

The gifts that land with that man share one quality: they ask nothing of him. No setup, no reading plan, no new habit. Here are twelve that pass that test, and the one we would put at the top.

## What works for the man who wants nothing

Three rules, learned the hard way:

- **It cannot create a chore.** A journal is a chore. A book is a chore. A thing that just sits on his wrist, his desk, or his truck dashboard is not.
- **It should say something you would not say out loud.** Most men in your life have never heard "I pray for you." A gift can carry that sentence for you.
- **It should be gone from the box by New Year's.** If it is still in the packaging on January 2, it was not the right gift.

## Twelve ideas

**1. A prayer band with a verse he would pick.** A silicone band with a verse on it, and a chip inside. When he taps it with his phone, it opens a page with a note from you and every prayer the family has said over it. He does not have to do anything. He just wears it, and when he taps it on a bad Tuesday, your note is there. More on this below.

**2. A letter, handwritten, about one specific thing he did.** Not "thanks for everything." One moment, one sentence of what it meant. He will keep it in his wallet.

**3. His verse, framed small, for the desk or the dash.** Not wall art. Something that fits where he actually spends his time.

**4. Coffee from where he grew up, or where he served.** The place matters more than the roast.

**5. A pocket knife or multi-tool with a verse reference on it.** Small engraving, big meaning. It goes in his pocket every day.

**6. Tickets, with you.** The gift is the afternoon, not the seat.

**7. A year of praying for him, written down.** A card that says "Every morning this year I am praying for you by name," and then do it. Tell him once in March that you did.

**8. A leather bookmark for the Bible he already has.** Nothing to learn, nothing to start.

**9. His name on a band he gives away.** Buy two bands, one for him and one with his name on the note, for him to hand to a friend. Some men would rather give than receive, and this lets him.

**10. A photo of his father or grandfather, printed properly.** Especially if he has only the phone version.

**11. The thing he keeps fixing, replaced.** The flashlight, the thermos, the work gloves. Notice what he nurses along, and buy the good one.

**12. A meal he loves that nobody makes anymore.** His mother's recipe, if you can find it.

## Why the band sits first

Most gifts on that list are one moment. The band is one moment that repeats.

- **It is on his wrist.** He does not have to remember it exists.
- **It carries your note.** Write it once at checkout. It opens every time he taps.
- **The family can pray over it all year.** Each of you taps Pray on his band's page, and when he opens it, he sees that eight people prayed over it this month. For a man who would never ask anyone to pray for him, that is a lot.
- **It does not announce anything.** A coworker sees a band with a verse on it. The rest is inside.

He will not say much when he opens it. That is fine. Watch his wrist in February.
