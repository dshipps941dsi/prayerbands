A prayer band is a wearable band with a small NFC chip sealed inside it. When someone holds a phone against the band, the phone opens the band's prayer page: a verse, the prayers attached to it, the people praying along. No app to download. No QR code to scan. No account required to see it.

That's the short version. The longer version is more interesting, because the tap isn't the point. The point is what happens after it.

## The problem a band solves

Prayer is mostly invisible. Someone says "I'll be praying for you," and then everyone walks away and nobody knows whether it happened, or who else is praying, or what changed.

That's not a failure of sincerity. It's a coordination problem. Prayer lives in individual heads and private moments, and there's no easy way to connect them without turning it into a group text nobody keeps up with.

A band puts something physical in the middle of that. It's on a wrist, so it's a reminder to the person wearing it. It's visible, so someone else can ask about it. And it's tappable, so the answer to "what is that?" isn't a five-minute explanation. It's a phone against a wrist and a page that opens.

## How the NFC part works

NFC stands for Near Field Communication. It's the same technology behind tap-to-pay and hotel key cards, and almost every phone made in the last several years has it built in.

Inside a prayer band there's a chip smaller than a fingernail with a thin antenna coil around it. The chip has no battery. It sits there inert until a phone comes within about an inch, and the phone's own NFC field powers it just long enough to hand over a single piece of information: a web address.

The phone shows a notification. Tap it, and the browser opens. That's the entire interaction.

**What this means practically:**

- **Nothing to charge.** The band has no battery because it doesn't need one.
- **Nothing to install.** It opens a web page, not an app.
- **Nothing to break.** The chip is sealed inside the silicone. Water, sweat, and washing don't reach it.
- **It works on a stranger's phone.** They don't need an account, a login, or anything installed. They tap, they see, they can pray right there.

### Which phones work

**iPhone:** iPhone XS and newer read NFC tags automatically with the screen on and unlocked. Just hold the top edge of the phone against the band. iPhone 7 through X can read them too, using the NFC Tag Reader button in Control Center.

**Android:** Most Android phones from the last several years support it. NFC is usually on by default; if nothing happens, look for NFC under Settings, usually in Connected devices or Connections. The tap point is usually the middle-to-upper back of the phone.

If a phone doesn't have NFC, nothing is lost. Every band has its ID printed on the inside and the site address on the outside, so the page can be reached by typing it in.

## What opens when you tap

The band doesn't just open a homepage. It opens that band's own page, which is the part that makes it a prayer band and not a novelty.

**A daily verse.** Chosen for the day, one screen, no essay.

**The band's prayers.** What the person wearing it is carrying right now, if they've chosen to share it, and the prayers others have left on it. This is the part that turns a tap into an actual prayer instead of a nice moment.

**A prayer circle.** A private group, a family, a team, a small group, that can see each other's requests and mark that they've prayed. [More on circles here.](/prayer-circles)

**The map.** Where a band has traveled, and where else in the world bands are being registered. It's the piece nobody expects to care about and almost everyone does, because it turns a private act into visible evidence that you're not doing it alone.

## Prayer band vs. regular faith bracelet

The obvious question: why not just a normal silicone bracelet with a verse printed on it?

A printed bracelet does one job well. It reminds the person wearing it. That's genuinely valuable, and if that's all you need, a printed band is a fine and inexpensive choice.

A prayer band does the same job, and then adds two things a printed band structurally can't:

**It's answerable.** Someone asks about the verse on a printed band and the wearer has to explain, out loud, on the spot. A lot of people, teenagers especially, won't. With a tap, the band explains itself.

**It changes.** A printed verse is the same verse for the life of the band. A tapped page updates: a new verse, current requests, whatever the wearer's circle is walking through this week. The band on the wrist stays the same; what it opens doesn't.

## Who wears them

**Athletes and teams.** A band survives practice, silicone is often allowed where jewelry isn't, and a whole roster wearing the same one turns into a team thing fast. [More on bands for athletes.](/blog/faith-gifts-for-teen-athletes)

**Families.** Especially spread-out ones: kids at college, a parent traveling, a grandparent three states away. The circle is the point; the band is how you get into it.

**Small groups and youth groups.** A group buys a set, everyone joins the same circle, and the weekly "how can we pray for you" question has an answer that persists past Wednesday night.

**People carrying something heavy.** A diagnosis in the family, a deployment, a hard season. The band is a way to let people in without having to retell the story every time someone asks how you're doing.

## Common questions

**Do I need an app?**
No. The band opens a web page in whatever browser is already on the phone. That's true for you and for anyone who taps your band.

**Is it waterproof?**
Yes. The chip is sealed inside silicone. Showers, pools, rain, and sweat are all fine.

**Can someone tap my band without me knowing?**
They can tap it; that's the design. But they only see what you've chosen to make visible. Private prayers and circle content stay private.

**Does it track my location?**
No. The band has no battery, no GPS, and no way to send anything on its own. The map shows the city a person typed when they registered a band, not where the band is. Full details are in the [FAQ](/faq).

**What if the person tapping isn't religious?**
Then they see a verse and a prayer, and they can do whatever they want with that. Nobody gets signed up for anything. Some of the best conversations start exactly there.

**How long does a band last?**
The silicone outlasts the season. The chip has no battery and no moving parts, so there's nothing to wear out.
