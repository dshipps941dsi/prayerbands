Ten bands under the tree is a lovely idea and a common failure. Here is what separates the family where everyone is still wearing theirs in February from the family where they went in a drawer on December 26.

## The mistake

The band gets handed over like a stocking stuffer. "Here, it is a prayer band, you tap it." The person tries to tap it in a loud room, nothing happens, and that is the last time they try.

A prayer band is a small thing, but it needs a moment. Give it one.

## Before Christmas

**Write the note at checkout.** Every band can carry a name and a personal note, and the note is what opens when they tap. Do not write "Merry Christmas." Write the thing you would say to that one person if you had thirty seconds and nobody else in the room. "I pray for you every morning on my drive. This is so you know." That note is the gift. The band is how it gets delivered.

**Order early.** Standard bands ship within a few days, but custom engraved ones take four to six weeks. For Christmas, order by early December for standard and the first week of November for custom.

**Tap your own first.** Register one for yourself before Christmas so you know exactly how the tap works on an iPhone and an Android. You will be the one showing everyone.

## The moment

Hand the bands out last, after the other gifts, when the room is quieter. Then do this:

1. **Say what it is in one sentence.** "It is a band with a verse. When you tap it with your phone, there is a note from me inside."
2. **Tap yours and show them the page.** Thirty seconds. They see the note, the verse, the prayers.
3. **Help each person tap theirs.** iPhone: hold the band to the top back of the phone. Android: the middle of the back. Some phones need a second try. Stay with it.
4. **Let them read their note alone.** Do not ask what it said.

That is it. Everyone in the room now has a band that works and a note they have read.

## After Christmas

**Pray over their bands.** This is the part nobody tells you. Open each person's band page and tap Pray. Once a week, on your drive, in January. When they open their band, they see the count climb. You never have to say a word, and they know.

**Make it a family circle, if it fits.** A prayer circle is a private group with a leader and one shared prayer wall. For a family it works like a group text that is only prayers. You post "Pray for Dad's scan on Thursday," and everyone taps Pray from wherever they are. The band on the wrist is how people remember to open it.

**Expect a few drawers anyway.** Two out of ten will stop wearing it. That is fine. The eight who keep it on are praying for the two.

## For a church giving bands to the congregation

The same rules scale. Put the note on the band from the pastor. Demonstrate the tap from the stage, once, slowly. Have ushers at the doors to help people tap on the way out, because half the room will not get it to work on the first try in the pew. Then post on the church's circle the week after Christmas so the bands open to something new.
