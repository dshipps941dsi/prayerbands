You already know which chair you are dreading. The brother-in-law with opinions. The parent who still brings up the thing. The cousin who will not be sober by four. Christmas dinner is three weeks away and you have already rehearsed the argument.

Here is a different kind of preparation: four weeks of praying for the people at that table, by name, before you see them. Not so they change. So you arrive different.

## The plan

Start the week after Thanksgiving. One week per group, one name per day.

**Week one: the easy ones.** The people you are glad to see. Pray for them first because it is easy and it warms up the habit. "God, be with Mom's knees this winter."

**Week two: the ones who are hurting.** Someone at the table lost a job, a spouse, a diagnosis came back. Pray for the thing they are carrying into the room. You will see them differently when they walk in.

**Week three: the hard ones.** Here is the week. One name a day, the ones you are dreading. The rule is simple: you may not pray that they change. You may pray for their good. "God, give him a good night's sleep. Give her peace about her son. Let him feel welcome." Say it out loud if you can. By the fourth day it gets easier. By the seventh you will notice something has shifted, and it is not them.

**Week four: yourself.** The last week before Christmas, pray for your own part. Your tongue. Your patience with the story you have heard nine times. The grace to let the comment go. Then one more time, every name at the table, in a row.

## What changes

Nothing about them, usually. What changes is that you walk in having said their name to God twenty times in a month, and it is hard to be contemptuous of someone you have been praying for. The argument you rehearsed does not happen because you are no longer standing where it starts.

Some years something does change in them. You will not be able to tell which year that is in advance, so pray every year.

## The band on the wrist

The plan fails on the days you forget, and December is made of days you forget. A prayer band is a silicone band with a verse on it and a chip inside, and it works for this in a plain way:

- **The band is the reminder.** You see it when you reach for the coffee. One name, one sentence, done.
- **Tap it to see who else prayed.** If your spouse or a friend is praying through the same list, you both tap Pray on the same page and see the count climb. Praying for the hard relative is easier with company.
- **Wear it to dinner.** Nobody at the table knows what it is for. You do.

If someone at the table has a band of their own, the kindest thing you can do in week three is tap Pray on theirs. They will see it, and they will not know it was you.
