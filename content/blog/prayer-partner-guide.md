A prayer partner is one person who prays for you, and whom you pray for, on purpose and on a schedule. Not a small group. Not a prayer chain. One person, both directions.

It sounds almost too simple to write about. But most people who want one don't have one, and most who start don't make it past the first month. Here is how to do both.

## Who to ask

The best prayer partner is usually not your closest friend. Close friends already know everything, and the conversation drifts into catching up. Look one ring out:

- someone from your small group or Bible study you'd like to know better
- a coworker or neighbor you already know is a believer
- a relative in a different stage of life (a niece, an uncle)
- someone your own age at church you keep meaning to talk to

The only real requirements: they take prayer seriously, and you'd be comfortable telling them one true thing about your week.

## How to ask

Say exactly what you mean, and make it small:

> "I'm trying to pray for one person on purpose this fall. Would you be my prayer partner? It's just this: we each share one thing a week, and we pray for it every day. I'll go first."

Two things make that easy to say yes to. It's bounded, one thing a week. And you're going first, so they don't have to be vulnerable before you are.

## What you actually do

Keep the structure light enough to survive a bad week.

**Once a week, swap one request each.** Text is fine. A voice memo is fine. Coffee is great but not required. The request should be specific: "the meeting Thursday with my boss," not "work stuff."

**Every day, pray for their one thing.** Twenty seconds counts. Attach it to something you already do each day so it doesn't depend on remembering. Many people use a physical reminder they wear or handle every morning; a Prayer Band works this way, and tapping it takes you straight to your partner's name.

**Once a week, tell them you did.** "Prayed for Thursday every morning this week." That single sentence is most of what makes a prayer partnership feel different from praying alone. If you use the band, tapping **Pray** beside their name on the Partners tab sends it with a note, and each of you can see a running chain of what's been sent and received.

**Once a month, ask what happened.** Follow up on last month's requests. The answered ones become the reason you both keep going.

## Guard the two things that kill it

**Scope creep.** The moment it becomes "let's also do a devotional together and read a chapter a day," it will die, because now it needs a free hour instead of a free minute. Add nothing for the first three months.

**Silence after a miss.** Someone will forget a week. When it happens, don't go quiet and don't apologize at length. Just send the next request. A partnership that can absorb missed weeks is one that lasts years.

## If you're doing this at church

A prayer partner program is one of the simplest things a congregation can run, and one of the few that reaches people who never join a small group. What works:

- **Pair people yourself** rather than asking them to find a partner. Most won't.
- **Pair across, not within.** Different ages and stages make for better prayers and better perspective.
- **Set an end date.** "Through Advent" or "for the eight weeks of Lent." People commit to seasons. They avoid forever. Partners who want to continue will, and they'll say so.
- **Give them a shared reminder.** Something both people wear or see daily, so the pairing has a physical presence. That's part of why churches hand out bands in pairs.

## Start this week

You don't need a program to begin. Pick one name from the list above, send the message, and share your first request before they reply with theirs. By Sunday you'll have prayed for one specific thing in someone's life seven times. That is already more than most of us managed last month.
