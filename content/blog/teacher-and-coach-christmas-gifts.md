Your kid's teacher will receive eleven mugs this December. The coach will get four. The youth pastor will get a candle and a card signed by people he cannot place.

They say thank you and they mean it. They also quietly wish for something that says a specific family noticed them. Here is a list that does, all under $25, and the one at the top that keeps saying it through May.

## What teachers and coaches actually keep

Ask one and they will tell you: the handwritten note from the kid, the thing that was clearly chosen for them, and anything that makes a Tuesday in February easier. Everything below hits at least one of those.

## Twelve gifts

**1. A prayer band with their name on the note.** A silicone band with a verse on it and a chip inside. Your family writes the note at checkout: "We pray for you by name every Sunday night." They tap it with their phone and read it. In March, when the class is wild and the season is lost, they tap it again and it is still there. More below.

**2. The note from the kid, in the kid's handwriting.** Attached to anything else on this list. This is the part they keep.

**3. A gift card to the coffee place by the school.** Not the big chain. The one they actually go to.

**4. Good hand cream.** Teachers wash their hands forty times a day.

**5. A bag of the good pens.** The ones that write smoothly and that students steal.

**6. A cold-weather kit for the coach.** Hand warmers, a beanie, a thermos. For the parent sidelines in January.

**7. A classroom wish-list item, the real one.** Most teachers have a list somewhere. Ask. Buy the thing at the top of it.

**8. A framed team or class photo.** Printed, not texted.

**9. A book of short devotions for a desk.** Short being the point. Two minutes before the bell.

**10. A meal, delivered on a weeknight in January.** The gift is the night off, and the timing says you were still thinking of them after Christmas.

**11. A thank-you from the whole team, with every name.** One card, every kid's first name, no parent handwriting. Coaches keep these for years.

**12. A second band, to give away.** Give the band, and a second one with a note: "For a student or player you are praying for." Youth leaders in particular know exactly who gets it.

## Why the band is first

The gift problem with teachers and coaches is that the relationship outlasts December. The band does too.

- It goes on the wrist and stays. Nothing to set up or remember.
- Your note opens every time they tap it. Write the thing you would say if you had thirty seconds alone with them.
- Your family can pray over it all year. Tap Pray on their band's page on Sunday nights. They open it and see that your family did, again. For a coach having the worst season of his life, that is not small.
- It does not announce anything in the hallway. A colleague sees a band with a verse.
